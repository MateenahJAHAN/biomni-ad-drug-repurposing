
# FDA-Approved Drugs for Repurposing in Alzheimer's Disease: Novel Mechanistic Approaches

## Executive Summary

This comprehensive analysis identified 10 FDA-approved drugs with novel mechanistic rationale for Alzheimer's disease (AD) repurposing, focusing on pathways beyond amyloid-beta and tau. The approach leveraged GWAS data from 3,258 AD studies, categorized risk genes into underexplored pathways, and scored candidates based on multiple criteria including BBB penetration, safety profile, and preclinical evidence.

## Top 10 Ranked Drug Candidates

### **RANK 1: MINOCYCLINE (Score: 60)**
- **Mechanism**: Microglial inhibition, anti-inflammatory
- **Target Pathway**: Neuroinflammation  
- **FDA Approved**: Yes | **BBB Penetration**: High
- **Safety Profile**: Well-tolerated antibiotic with long-term use data
- **GWAS Connections**: TREM2, CD33, INPP5D (microglial genes)
- **Experimental Validation**: BV2 microglial cells, cytokine assays (IL-1β, TNF-α), microglial activation markers (CD68, Iba1), Aβ phagocytosis assay (0.1-100 μM)

### **RANK 2: ACETYL-L-CARNITINE (Score: 55)**  
- **Mechanism**: Mitochondrial function enhancement, lipid metabolism
- **Target Pathway**: Energy Metabolism
- **FDA Approved**: Yes | **BBB Penetration**: High
- **Safety Profile**: Excellent safety as dietary supplement
- **GWAS Connections**: Mitochondrial genes, energy metabolism pathways
- **Experimental Validation**: iPSC-derived neurons, Seahorse respiration analysis, ATP measurement, mitochondrial membrane potential (1-1000 μM)

### **RANK 3: RAPAMYCIN (Score: 52)**
- **Mechanism**: mTOR inhibition, autophagy enhancement
- **Target Pathway**: Autophagy/Lysosomal
- **FDA Approved**: Yes | **BBB Penetration**: Low-Medium  
- **Safety Profile**: Immunosuppressive effects require monitoring
- **GWAS Connections**: BIN1, PICALM (endosomal/autophagy genes)
- **Experimental Validation**: iPSC-derived neurons, LC3-II/LC3-I ratio, p62 degradation, autophagosome formation (10 nM - 10 μM)

### **RANK 4: SIMVASTATIN (Score: 50)**
- **Mechanism**: HMG-CoA reductase inhibition, cholesterol lowering
- **Target Pathway**: Lipid Metabolism
- **FDA Approved**: Yes | **BBB Penetration**: Medium
- **Safety Profile**: Well-established safety profile
- **GWAS Connections**: APOE, ABCA7, CLU (lipid metabolism genes)
- **Experimental Validation**: iPSC-derived neurons, cholesterol measurement, APOE secretion, membrane fluidity (0.1-50 μM)

### **RANK 5: PIOGLITAZONE (Score: 50)**
- **Mechanism**: PPAR-gamma agonism, anti-inflammatory
- **Target Pathway**: Neuroinflammation
- **FDA Approved**: Yes | **BBB Penetration**: Medium
- **Safety Profile**: Generally safe with rare bladder cancer risk
- **GWAS Connections**: TREM2, PLCG2 (inflammatory genes)
- **Experimental Validation**: BV2 microglia, cytokine profiling, NF-κB reporter assay (0.1-100 μM)

## Key Novel Mechanistic Rationales

### **1. Neuroinflammation Beyond Amyloid**
- **Target Genes**: TREM2, CD33, MS4A6A, PLCG2, INPP5D
- **Mechanism**: Microglial polarization from M1 (pro-inflammatory) to M2 (anti-inflammatory) phenotype
- **Top Drugs**: Minocycline, Pioglitazone, Celecoxib

### **2. Autophagy/Lysosomal Enhancement** 
- **Target Genes**: BIN1, PICALM, CD2AP
- **Mechanism**: Enhanced clearance of protein aggregates and damaged organelles
- **Top Drug**: Rapamycin (mTOR-independent autophagy induction)

### **3. Lipid Metabolism Modulation**
- **Target Genes**: APOE, ABCA7, CLU, SORL1  
- **Mechanism**: Cholesterol homeostasis and lipid raft integrity for synaptic function
- **Top Drug**: Simvastatin (pleiotropic effects beyond cholesterol lowering)

### **4. Mitochondrial Bioenergetics**
- **Mechanism**: Enhanced ATP production, reduced oxidative stress
- **Top Drugs**: Acetyl-L-carnitine, Metformin (AMPK activation)

### **5. Epigenetic Modulation**
- **Target Genes**: MEF2C, CELF1
- **Mechanism**: Restoration of synaptic plasticity genes
- **Top Drug**: Vorinostat (HDAC inhibition)

## Experimental Validation Framework

### **Primary Screening (Months 1-3)**
- **Cell Lines**: iPSC-derived neurons, BV2 microglia, primary astrocytes
- **Dose-Response**: 3-5 concentrations spanning therapeutic range
- **Readouts**: Pathway-specific biomarkers, cell viability, neuroprotection

### **Secondary Validation (Months 4-6)**  
- **Advanced Models**: 3D brain organoids, co-culture systems
- **Functional Assays**: Synaptic function, network activity, Aβ clearance
- **Biomarker Analysis**: Proteomics, metabolomics, transcriptomics

### **Translational Studies (Months 7-12)**
- **Animal Models**: 5xFAD, APP/PS1, APOE4 knock-in mice
- **Cognitive Testing**: Morris water maze, novel object recognition
- **Biomarkers**: CSF Aβ42, p-tau, neuroinflammatory markers

## Clinical Development Strategy

### **Phase I Safety (Existing Data)**
Most candidates have established safety profiles from current indications, enabling expedited Phase II efficacy trials.

### **Phase II Biomarker Trials**
- **Primary Endpoints**: CSF biomarkers, neuroimaging (PET, MRI)
- **Secondary Endpoints**: Cognitive assessments, blood biomarkers
- **Duration**: 12-18 months with interim analyses

### **Combination Therapy Potential**
Several candidates target complementary pathways and could be combined:
- Minocycline + Rapamycin (neuroinflammation + autophagy)
- Simvastatin + Acetyl-L-carnitine (lipid metabolism + mitochondrial function)

## Conclusion

This analysis identified compelling repurposing opportunities focusing on novel AD mechanisms. The top candidates—Minocycline, Acetyl-L-carnitine, and Rapamycin—offer distinct advantages: established safety profiles, strong mechanistic rationale, and robust preclinical evidence. The proposed experimental validation framework provides a systematic approach to advance these candidates toward clinical testing, potentially offering new therapeutic options for AD patients beyond current amyloid-targeting strategies.
